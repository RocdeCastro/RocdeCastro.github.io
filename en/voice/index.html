How I design for voice

Three principles across the cases

1

Speech is not text read aloud

Speech gets rhythm, pauses and emphasis; the screen stays short and shows options. I write both versions of every turn.

With LLMs: the answer is designed for the person who reads or hears it, not to show what the model knows.

2

Every error has a way out

No-match and no-input are planned as an escalation: rephrase, give examples, offer an exit. Nobody gets stuck in a loop.

With LLMs: the model says it doesn't know, hands off to a human, and every hard case becomes a test.

3

Users come in through the middle

Not everyone goes through the menu. Implicit invocations and shortcuts take people straight to what they already know they want.

With LLMs: questions arrive in any shape, and retrieval has to find the right passage in the knowledge base.

2024–2026 — Itaú · Knowledge bases and RAG

Knowledge bases for RAG: prepare, curate and find the real bottleneck

1,132 curation hours saved in 2025 · 450,000+ interactions analyzed
Context
The contact center copilots answer from knowledge bases written for people: long scripts with images, tables and jargon. When an answer is wrong, the first suspect is usually the content.
Delivery
Prepare: the team I built and led created a curation pipeline with image transcription, HTML-to-Markdown conversion, synthetic questions and accuracy evaluation, to get the bases ready for RAG. Curate: copilot curation moved from samples to 100% of conversations, with human and synthetic curators. Review: a harness prototype in which the LLM writes the revised base and code checks that every line traces back to the original.
Outcome
1,132 curation hours saved in 2025 and 450,000+ interactions analyzed. The analysis showed 46.8% of failures came from retrieval, not content; with tests showing little impact from rewriting articles, priority moved to search and RAG parameter tuning.

2026 — Itaú · Contact center copilot

Answer-improvement prompts: staff read less and find more

11 of 12 agents in production · −39.8% estimated time per interaction · +26 pts completeness
Context
The AI agents that support contact center staff gave long, loosely structured answers that staff had to interpret with the customer on the line.
Delivery
Prompt re-engineering for 12 agents (insurance, auto, accounts and more), with a new answer format and paired tests of 256 to 1,007 question-answer pairs per agent before each release. I won over each squad with the tests and with research alongside contact center staff on the floor.
Outcome
−39.8% in estimated time per interaction and +26 points in completeness in the agents measured; 89.4% of the gain came from staff reading less, not from the model answering faster.

2020–2021 — Magalu · Lu on WhatsApp

Cancel on WhatsApp: Lu's transactional flow, from contact center immersion to continuous improvement

40,000 cancellations opened by the bot in 6 months · 7 to 18 turns on the happy path
Context
Lu already talked to millions of customers on WhatsApp through order-tracking messages. The next step was letting people cancel an order right there, without going through a human agent.
My role
Usability and conversational design specialist in the post-purchase squad, the first and only one in that role at Magalu at the time. I designed the flow following the company's UX framework end to end: understand, define, design, follow through and measure.
Delivery
Understand: four shadowing sessions with contact center staff across phone, email, chat and social media, four interviews with supervisors and managers, and a workshop with six squads. Define: a conversational workshop with five contact center staff role-playing real conversations, card sorting of customers' first messages, volume by cancellation type, and a heatmap of the “My orders” page, where almost half of the interactions were with the most recent order. Design: high-level flow, interactive prototype, remote usability tests and technical spec. Follow through: versioning and developer guides, up to Pix refunds. Measure: Watson continuous improvement notebooks to find ambiguous examples across intents and completion and abandonment rates per flow.
Outcome
40,000 cancellations opened by the WhatsApp bot in the first 6 months.

16 use cases · 4 intent families · 61,000+ interactions
Context
As a freelancer for MediaMonks, then a Google partner, I designed Amstel's “Artificial Fan” for the Copa Libertadores: an AI that “learned how to cheer” from Brazilian fans. It talked about the tournament, asked fans how to support a team and played back what it had learned, plus historical stats.

I wrote the App Proposal and the sample dialogs: persona, 16 use cases (from second screen at half-time to the “trolling hater”), intent architecture, conversational components, and the design for turning fans' answers into content.

From VUI to generative AI

What voice taught me about LLMs

The persona becomes the instruction

The profile that once guided the writer now guides the model: tone, limits and what never to say. From Nhac GNT to Itaú's tone of voice.

The sample dialog becomes the test

The sample dialogs that approved a flow became evaluation sets and paired tests before every release.

The repair ladder becomes the fallback

No-match and no-input became “I don't know” and a handoff to a human, with the fallback rate tracked as a metric.
